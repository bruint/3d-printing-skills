# P2S and PLA reference

Use these as starting points, then confirm the current printer, nozzle, filament, and slicer profiles for the actual job.

- The Bambu P2S has a **256 × 256 × 256 mm** build volume and a stock **0.4 mm nozzle**. Leave margin for bed adhesion and slicer-specific exclusions rather than planning a part at exactly 256 mm. [Bambu P2S announcement](https://blog.bambulab.com/the-icon-redefined-meet-the-p2s-a-completely-reengineered-version-of-the-ultra-productive-p1-series/).
- Bambu lists PLA Basic's heat deflection temperature around **57 °C** in its filament comparisons. That is a test value, not a safe continuous service temperature. PLA can creep under constant stress at room temperature, so test clamped or loaded parts over time. [Bambu filament comparison](https://au.store.bambulab.com/products/petg-hf?skr=yes), [PLA creep experiment](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4360/12/12/2962).
- Layer orientation can dominate flexural strength; one PLA experiment found it more influential than infill density and pattern for the tested specimens. Design and orient the part around the actual load, not a nominal bulk filament strength. [Experimental PLA bending study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6926899/).
- As a practical starting point with the stock nozzle, use the P2S and PLA Basic profiles, about **0.2 mm** layers, **4–5 wall loops**, and moderate infill. Increase local walls or add CAD ribs where force enters the part; inspect the sliced toolpaths. Wall loops often matter more than infill for shell-dominated parts. These settings are a starting hypothesis, not a universal strength recipe. [Prusa infill guidance](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/infill_42).
- Overhangs, fit clearance, and fillet direction depend on orientation. A bed-facing fillet can print poorly, while a chamfer often prints cleanly. Print a small fit sample when clearances matter. [Prusa modeling guidance](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135).
- Avoid sharp transitions at loaded roots and add local reinforcement where sections change. This is consistent with measured failure at geometric stress concentrations in printed PLA specimens; exact benefit depends on load and print orientation. [PLA geometry study](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4360/18/2/243).

## Minimalist form and texture

- For an understated functional part, make its purpose and hierarchy visible through a simple overall shape, aligned repeated features, restrained color, and few decorative breaks. Braun describes its product language in terms of order, clarity, simplicity, and function; Vitsœ presents Dieter Rams's principle of concentrating on essentials. Translate those ideas into a consistent silhouette and carefully placed details rather than a ban on curves or texture. [Braun design history](https://secure-us.braun.com/en-us/design/design-and-innovation), [Vitsœ principles](https://www.vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design).
- Chamfers, bevels, and corner rounding can make exposed edges read as intentional and feel better to handle. Use a small, consistent set on presentation surfaces; reserve larger structural radii for loaded inside corners. Check whether the chosen edge treatment creates a bed-facing overhang before committing to it. [Prusa modeling guidance](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135).
- Bambu says its Textured PEI Plate transfers a rugged texture to the print's first layer. This is a way to finish a broad face without modeling microtexture. The effect is limited to the bed-contact surface and depends on the plate; keep critical fits off that face or validate them with a coupon. [Bambu Textured PEI Plate](https://us.store.bambulab.com/collections/accessories-for-a1-mini/products/bambu-textured-pei-plate), [Prusa textured sheet](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/textured-steel-sheet_196534?product=ht90).
